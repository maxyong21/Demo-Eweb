First-meeting guide: six questions we’ll ask

We’ll speak with the owner, sales staff and stock staff, then a small sample of current, inactive and potential dealers.

  1. “When you say sales are unsatisfactory, what result did you expect?” Revenue, paid orders, profit, new dealers or repeat business, and over which period.
  2. “Show us a recent enquiry that became an order, and one that did not.” We follow the steps, timing, costs and reasons.
  3. “Which task is hard to manage today? Show us the last time it happened.” We check what Shopee already supports and what is actually missing.
  4. “Tell us about your last stock purchase from a supplier.” (to buyers) How they chose, compared total cost and checked availability.
  5. “What happened the last time you needed to buy again?” Demand, habits, service issues and alternative suppliers.
  6. “What would make this project worth its cost?” A baseline, a target, an owner and a review date.

Helpful to bring: about 90 days of orders and product performance, ad spend, discounts and fees, product costs, delivery and returns, stockouts, and a few anonymised enquiries or cancellations. If records are missing, we’ll start a simple enquiry log.

Why we look at these areas
  • Consistency and support matter. McKinsey’s May 2026 B2B research links inconsistent information and weak support to suppliers losing customers. Read the research ↗
  • Shopee already has wholesale prices. Other promotions can temporarily hide them, so we audit the current shop first. Shopee’s guidance ↗
  • Real stories beat hypothetical approval. GOV.UK recommends open questions about actual experiences. Read the method ↗

These sources guide the investigation. They are not evidence about Lucky Dotcom or a forecast of Malaysian wholesale sales.

What makes a useful pilot
  • Agree the test first. Record today’s result, the target, who owns it and a review date.
  • Separate ease of use from sales impact. A few buyers trying tasks shows friction, not a sales forecast. Real orders need secure accounts and a shared database first.
  • Compare fairly. Tag pilot orders and note changes in ads, stock, prices and season.
  • Count new costs too. Include acquisition, payments, maintenance and returns.
  • Be ready to choose another fix. If the issue is product, price, supply or staff capacity, fix that first.
Common questions

Will a website automatically increase sales? No. It can remove friction and improve follow-up, but it still needs good products, competitive offers and a way to attract buyers.

Will we stop using Shopee? No. Keep improving Shopee, and build the direct channel through your own marketing.

Can our team manage the website? The demo already lets an admin manage products, stock and orders. A real project adds staff access, training, backups and maintenance.

Is this connected to our business? Not yet. The demo uses fictional data. Payments, stock integration and messaging would be built separately.

What should we prepare? Your product catalogue, wholesale rules, delivery and warranty terms, recent order and margin data, and the stock or accounting systems you use.
