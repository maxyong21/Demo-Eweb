Use this guide in the first client meeting

Start with a recent example. Ask the owner, sales staff and stock/fulfilment staff to show how an order actually happens. Then speak with a small initial sample of current, inactive and prospective dealers. Interviews are exploratory, not a representative survey.

  1. “When you say sales are unsatisfactory, what result did you expect?” Agree whether the concern is revenue, paid orders, profit, new dealers or repeat business—and the period being compared.
  2. “Show us a recent enquiry that became an order, and one that did not.” Follow the steps, timestamps, costs and recorded reasons. Separate observations from guesses.
  3. “Which task is difficult to manage today? Show us the last time it happened.” Establish what Shopee or the current process already supports and what is actually missing.
  4. “Tell us about your last stock purchase from a supplier.” Ask buyers how they selected the supplier, compared total cost, checked availability and handled questions.
  5. “What happened the last time you needed to buy again?” Look for product demand, replenishment habits, service issues and alternative suppliers before assuming reminders are the solution.
  6. “What would make this project worth its cost?” Agree a baseline, a practical target, an owner and a review date before choosing the pilot scope.

Bring what is available: ideally 90 days of order and product performance, with a longer comparison where possible; advertising spend; seller-funded discounts and actual fees; landed product costs; delivery subsidies and returns; stockouts; and a small anonymised sample of enquiries, quotations and cancellations. If records are missing, begin with a simple enquiry/order log.

Observe before demonstrating. Hear how buyers work without leading them toward a website. Then ask them to try a relevant task in the demo and explain what remains difficult.

Why investigate these areas?

Consistency and support matter. McKinsey’s May 2026 global B2B research highlights inconsistent information and lack of knowledgeable support as drivers of supplier switching. This supports examining the whole buying journey. Read the research ↗

Wholesale prices already exist on Shopee. Its help page says other promotions can temporarily hide wholesale prices. Audit the current shop before assuming a new channel is required. Read Shopee’s guidance ↗

Real examples are better than hypothetical approval. GOV.UK’s interview guidance recommends open questions and stories about actual experiences. Use that method to establish needs before showing proposed features. Read the method ↗

Candidate scope, subject to discovery

What each side could use.

“In demo” means a working browser-based example. “To build” means a proposed capability. Neither label means the customer has confirmed a need for it.

For your admin team

Less manual work. Clearer control.

  • In demoManage products and orders

    Edit prices and stock, view customers and update order status.

  • To buildUpload product photos

    Add multiple images, preview them, choose a cover photo, reorder or remove images, and edit specifications. Help buyers assess products with less messaging.

  • In demoApprove dealers and set wholesale rules

    Review applications and approve, decline or revoke dealer access. Set each product's minimum units, pack increments and quantity prices, plus an optional dealer order minimum. Retail customers remain unrestricted by dealer minimums.

  • To buildHandle quotations and payments

    Receive bulk enquiries, issue quotes and record acceptance. Verify payment separately from fulfilment and add actual tracking details.

  • In demoExplore order performance

    Filter by date and status, inspect daily results and categories, and export reports. Spot products and orders needing attention.

  • To buildMeasure profit and keep channels in sync

    Add product costs, campaign sources and promotion performance. Connect shared stock across the website, sales team and Shopee to reduce overselling.

For wholesale buyers

Less uncertainty. Easier ordering.

  • In demoBrowse and place a sample order

    Search products, adjust cart quantities, use sample checkout and view order history.

  • In demoApply for a dealer account

    Receive an optional invitation above 100 units, submit business details and wait for admin approval. Continue buying at retail prices while pending; dealer prices activate only after approval.

  • In demoUnderstand bulk prices before ordering

    See dealer minimums, pack increments and live quantity prices. Example: RM 1 retail; approved dealers pay RM 45 for 50 units or RM 85 for 100. Cart and checkout enforce the configured rules. Production delivery terms still need confirmation.

  • To buildOrder many products or request a quote

    Enter quantities in a quick order table, or submit one enquiry containing all products. Review and accept the admin team's quotation.

  • To buildBuy again with fewer steps

    Rebuild a previous order with current stock and pricing checks. Receive relevant stock alerts and reminders only after opting in.

  • To buildPay, track and get help

    Use connected payments, see payment and shipment updates, and send product-linked WhatsApp enquiries. Access clear warranty and return procedures.

One connected workflow

Dealer applies → admin approves and assigns prices → buyer orders or requests a quote → admin confirms payment and fulfils → buyer tracks and reorders.

Production also needs secure accounts, a shared database, image storage, staff permissions, backups and training. Scope and costs must be confirmed before implementation.

The decision that matters

Is the business better off?

Compare extra contribution and staff time saved against the website's build, running and acquisition costs. Count orders moving from Shopee only for the actual change in contribution.

✓ More qualified buying enquiries✓ More customers ordering again✓ Fewer mistakes and less admin✓ Better contribution after costs

What would count as a useful pilot?

  • Agree the test before starting. Record the current result, the intended improvement, the responsible person and a review date. Choose targets from the baseline rather than borrowing an industry percentage.
  • Separate usability from commercial impact. A few buyers can test sample tasks such as finding a product and understanding a bulk price. This reveals friction; it does not establish a sales forecast. Real orders require secure production accounts, a shared database and server-side checks first.
  • Compare fairly. Tag pilot orders and track comparable products and buyers. Record changes in advertising, stock, prices and seasonality. A small before/after comparison is directional evidence, not proof that the website alone caused a change.
  • Count both benefits and new costs. Measure completed paid orders, contribution and handling time. Include acquisition, payments, maintenance and returns. Moving an order from Shopee is not automatically a new sale.
  • Be willing to choose another fix. If the main issue is product demand, supplier reliability, price, fulfilment or staff capacity, address that first. High minimum quantities and slow dealer approval could also discourage buyers; validate these demo rules.

Do not expand to a full platform until a meaningful problem, a usable solution and a plausible operating model are supported by evidence.

A few practical questions

Clear expectations
from the start.

Will a website automatically increase sales?

No. It can remove buying friction and improve follow-up, but it still needs suitable products, competitive offers, reliable service and a way to attract buyers. We test and measure these together.

Will we stop using Shopee?

No. Improve Shopee while building your direct channel through your own marketing and independently acquired enquiries. Keep Shopee transactions on Shopee.

Can our team manage the website?

The existing demo lets an administrator manage products, stock and orders. A production project should include staff access, training, backups, clear ownership and a maintenance arrangement.

Is this already connected to our business?

No. This is a browser-based demonstration with fictional catalogue and order data. Payments, inventory integrations and automated messaging require a separate implementation. Demo logins are for presentation only.

What should we prepare before starting?

Your verified product catalogue, wholesale rules, delivery and warranty terms, recent order and margin data, and details of the stock/accounting systems you use. These determine the scope and a realistic quotation.
